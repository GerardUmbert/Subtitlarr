Ollama defaults to 4096 regardless of what the model supports — long or verbose (e.g. dual-language, heavily formatted) subtitle content can exceed that and get silently truncated mid-response. Raise this if translations of longer or denser files come back incomplete. Higher values use more RAM.

How many dialogue tokens go into one translation request. Small models can lose reliable formatting on a big batch even though it technically fits the context window — a batch that fits isn't a batch the model can format correctly.

0 = auto (derives the batch size from the context window above — usually the LARGEST setting, not the smallest). 1 = one cue per request, the most reliable option but the slowest, since per-request overhead adds up. 400–900 is the sweet spot — all land at roughly the same per-cue speed, but 900 has been seen to drop into repeated/garbled output on some content, while 400 has not. 400 (default) is the safer pick; raise it once you've confirmed your content is fine at a bigger size.

Seeing untranslated lines or a "recovered N/M cues" failure? Lower this value.

How many batches are sent to NVIDIA at once before waiting for that group to finish and starting the next. NVIDIA's free tier allows up to 40 requests/minute — since each request typically takes several seconds, a small number here naturally stays well under that limit without needing to be a literal requests-per-minute counter.
